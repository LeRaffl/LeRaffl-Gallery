The fit is provisional: its 80% date moved by 6 years across recent re-fits. The band is for the fitted line, point by point, and holds only if the transition keeps following an S-shape. How the bands are calculated.

The gallery fits a generalized Weibull curve to the BEV share of each period: share(t) = 1 − exp(v1 · (t − t0)v2), t in calendar years. For Hungary: v1 = −3.495 × 10−3, v2 = 1.511, t0 = 2017, fitted through September 2026, model run 2026-10-22. This is the project's own model, not an official forecast. A policy change or a stall is not in it.

Each new or changed month re-renders the country. Shares are read from the CSV as column ÷ TOTAL and grouped into three curves. About the ACEA figures

This country's monthly total comes from ACEA's Europe-wide new-car-registrations press release — one PDF covering the EU, EFTA and the UK, parsed into per-country rows. It is an industry-association aggregate, not a direct national-registry feed: it counts registrations reported through ACEA, is published with a lag (usually the third to fourth week of the following month), and carries only the fuel split ACEA itself reports. Where a national registry is the richer source of record (e.g. Norway's OFV), we still ingest the ACEA figure here for consistency across the cluster.

Caveats

  • An industry-association aggregate, not a national-registry feed — see the note above for what that means for completeness and timing.
  • The six fuel columns are the ones ACEA itself prints (battery electric, plug-in hybrid, hybrid electric, others, petrol, diesel). OTHERS is a reported column, not a residual we compute, and it is where LPG, CNG and hydrogen end up.
  • Each release also restates the same month a year earlier, so a prior-year figure can be corrected retroactively.
