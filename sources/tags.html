How the tags work

Every gallery card carries up to three small tags — Status, Speed and Timing — read off the market's fitted S-curve. Status is absolute (80 % share and the year 2035). Speed and Timing are relative — a market is compared with the other markets of its vehicle type, so trucks are measured against trucks, not against cars.

Reference groups

Only base variants define a scale. For cars that is the main new-car curve (Whole) of every market, one per country. Car subsets — private and company keepers, rental and non-rental, used imports, and other car slices — do not shape the scale; they are placed on Whole's scale, like a dot on Whole's boxplot.

Vans, trucks (HDV), buses, two- and three-wheelers and so on are their own reference groups. A subset of one of them is compared against that parent — Canada's pickup trucks, for example, are placed on the Vans scale.

A reference group needs at least 4 markets. Smaller groups — and their subsets — get no speed or timing tag.

Filtering the gallery by tag

Right under the search box, country and chart types, the gallery offers the tags themselves as chips, in four groups — Fit (Reliable, Unreliable, No transition), Status (Past 80 %, On track, Late), Speed (Fast, Avg, Slow) and Start (Early, Mid, Late). Start is the Timing tag; Mid is the middle half, which carries no pill on the cards.

Nothing picked shows everything. Within a group any picked tag matches; across groups all must match — Fast and Early means fast and early, Fast and Avg means either.

Every chart has exactly one Fit value. No-transition charts appear only under No transition, never as Reliable, and Unreliable or No-transition charts carry no Status, Speed or Start — they drop out as soon as one of those is picked. So does a market whose group is too small for a scale, once a Speed or Start value is picked; it is never counted as Mid.

Each chip shows how many charts it would give with the other groups' picks applied; chips that would give none are dimmed. Picked tags also appear as removable chips above the results.

The three tags

Status — where the fitted curve crosses 80 % BEV share of new registrations. Past 80 % (already crossed), On track (by the end of 2035), Late (after 2035). The same for every vehicle type.

Speed — the slope of the fitted curve at its steepest point. Fast = top quarter of the reference group, Slow = bottom quarter, Avg speed = the middle half.

Timing — the year the fitted curve crosses 10 % BEV share. Markets still below 10 % count with their projected year. Early starter = earliest quarter of the reference group, Late starter = latest quarter; the middle half carries no timing tag.

Curves that show no transition get a single No transition tag, and fits that cannot be trusted yet (unsettled, collapsed or stale) a single Unreliable fit tag; neither counts towards any scale.

How a tag is decided

Read every fitted curve

One row per market and variant from params.csv (the S-curve parameters, the latest data month, the observed BEV share and the re-fit stability).

▼
Can the curve carry tags?
Does the curve show a transition?
  • no — no S-shape, or the peak adoption rate stays negligible→No transition (single tag, not in any scale)
  • yes
    Is the fit reliable?
    • no — unsettled, collapsed or stale→Unreliable fit (single tag, not in any scale)
    • provisional — moved 5–25 years across re-fits→tags drawn hollow, counts in its scale
    • yes→tags solid, counts in its scale
▼
Pick the reference group
Is the variant a subset of another one (TAG_PARENT)?
  • Private, Industry, Rental, NonRental, Used, Custom, Wholesale, 4-Wheelers, …→Whole (classified on Whole's scale, does not shape it)
  • Pickups→Vans (classified on the Vans scale)
  • no — a base variant (Whole, Vans, HDV, Buses, 2-Wheelers, …)→its own group (shapes the scale, one row per country)

Build each group's scale
At least 4 markets in the reference group?
  • no→no speed / timing tag for the group and its subsets
  • yes→25th and 75th percentile of the 10 % year and of the speed
