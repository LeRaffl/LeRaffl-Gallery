The fit is provisional: its 80% date moved by 9 years across recent re-fits. The band is for the fitted line, point by point, and holds only if the transition keeps following an S-shape. How the bands are calculated.

The gallery fits a generalized Weibull curve to the BEV share of each period: share(t) = 1 − exp(v1 · (t − t0)v2), t in calendar years. For Australia: v1 = −1.240 × 10−6, v2 = 4.757, t0 = 2014, fitted through October 2026, model run 2026-11-09. This is the project's own model, not an official forecast. A policy change or a stall is not in it.

Where the numbers come from

New light-vehicle sales for Australia from VFACTS, with a curated historical series.

How it works

Australia's monthly figures come from VFACTS, the industry sales report administered by the FCAI (Federal Chamber of Automotive Industries), with a longer historical series curated by Prof. Ray Willis. Entered by hand.

Not automatable from free sources (checked 2026-10-09): the FCAI's monthly media release (fcai.com.au, readable through its public WordPress API) gives the battery-electric share and growth rates in prose and only class, brand, model and state tables as images — the counts by powertrain are in the paid VFACTS report. See docs/architecture/14-data-source-gaps.md.

Render

Each new or changed month re-renders the country. Shares are read from the CSV as column ÷ TOTAL and grouped into three curves. A generalized Weibull S-curve is fitted to the BEV and the ICE share, and the charts, the fitted parameters, the twelve-month totals and the uncertainty bands are rewritten.

Which curve does a CSV column count towards?
  • BEV→BEV
  • PHEV or EREV (plug-in hybrids, range extenders)
    Does the source split plug-ins from other hybrids?
    • yes→PHEV
    • no — one combined hybrid figure (parked in HEV)→ICE
      the PHEV curve is left out, never drawn at zero
  • HEV or MHEV (full and mild hybrids — no plug)→ICE
  • petrol, diesel, gas, flex-fuel, others→ICE
