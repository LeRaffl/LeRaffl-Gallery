The band is for the fitted line, point by point, and holds only if the transition keeps following an S-shape. How the bands are calculated.

The gallery fits a generalized Weibull curve to the BEV share of each period: share(t) = 1 − exp(v1 · (t − t0)v2), t in calendar years. For Netherlands: v1 = −1.143 × 10−4, v2 = 3.096, t0 = 2010, fitted through October 2026, model run 2026-11-21. This is the project's own model, not an official forecast. A policy change or a stall is not in it.

Render

Each new or changed month re-renders the country. Shares are read from the CSV as column ÷ TOTAL and grouped into three curves. A generalized Weibull S-curve is fitted to the BEV and the ICE share, and the charts, the fitted parameters, the twelve-month totals and the uncertainty bands are rewritten.

Which curve does a CSV column count towards?
  • BEV→BEV
  • PHEV or EREV (plug-in hybrids, range extenders)
    Does the source split plug-ins from other hybrids?
    • yes→PHEV
    • no — one combined hybrid figure (parked in HEV)→ICE
      the PHEV curve is left out, never drawn at zero
  • HEV or MHEV (full and mild hybrids — no plug)→ICE
  • petrol, diesel, gas, flex-fuel, others→ICE

Caveats

  • Swing doesn't split hybrids out of petrol. For Whole from 2023-07 its petrol is split by the HEV share the RDW register counts record by record; the share, not the register's absolute counts, because cars exported or scrapped since have left the register.
  • 2018-01 to 2023-06 HEV is an estimate: Swing petrol × the register's HEV share of HEV + petrol in the month (reference/nl_register_fuel_split.csv). 2011–2017 rows have no petrol/diesel split, so their HEV = TOTAL × the register's HEV share of all the month's cars, and petrol and diesel stay blank.
  • HEV is RDW's NOVC-HEV class from the type approval, full and mild hybrids together (as ACEA's HEV, and as Switzerland's and Estonia's register HEV here). Checked 2026-11-21 by electric power, under 25 kW being a mild hybrid. That was 19% of the class in 2019-06, 54% in 2022-06 and 40% in 2026-09 (Suzuki, Mazda, Audi 12 V, VW eTSI, Ford EcoBoost Hybrid, the Stellantis 48 V Hybrid). ACEA's 2018–2019 NL count did not include them yet: the register runs 13–30% above it then, within 5% from 2020.
  • Publication date varies within the first half of the month.
  • The portal blocks GitHub Actions and Cloudflare IPs; data is fetched via a Deno Deploy relay.
  • Printed under the charts: New passenger cars. HEV = full and mild hybrids (the EU type-approval class, as ACEA counts them): petrol split by the RDW register's hybrid share from 2023-07, an estimate on the same basis before (2011-2017 from the share of all cars).
