The band is for the fitted line, point by point, and holds only if the transition keeps following an S-shape. How the bands are calculated.

The gallery fits a generalized Weibull curve to the BEV share of each period: share(t) = 1 − exp(v1 · (t − t0)v2), t in calendar years. For Germany: v1 = −6.482 × 10−5, v2 = 3.102, t0 = 2011, fitted through October 2026, model run 2026-11-04. This is the project's own model, not an official forecast. A policy change or a stall is not in it.

Where the numbers come from

New passenger-car registrations for Germany from the Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt (KBA).

How it works

The KBA (Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt) is Germany's federal motor-transport authority and the official vehicle register. It publishes monthly new-registration counts (Neuzulassungen) broken down by fuel type. Because it's the registry itself, coverage is complete — every registered car, not an association sample.

Germany is not pulled by the ACEA fetcher (which skips it). Its monthly figures come from the KBA press-release '…_merkmale.xlsx' workbook via scripts/fetch_germany.py (workflow fetch-germany.yml); OTHERS is the residual TOTAL − BEV − PHEV − HEV − PETROL − DIESEL (LPG, CNG, hydrogen, fuel-cell and any unlisted 'Sonstige'). Early rows (pre-automation, quarterly/modelled decimals) are left as-is.

Render

Each new or changed month re-renders the country. Shares are read from the CSV as column ÷ TOTAL and grouped into three curves. A generalized Weibull S-curve is fitted to the BEV and the ICE share, and the charts, the fitted parameters, the twelve-month totals and the uncertainty bands are rewritten.

Which curve does a CSV column count towards?
  • BEV→BEV
  • PHEV or EREV (plug-in hybrids, range extenders)
    Does the source split plug-ins from other hybrids?
    • yes→PHEV
    • no — one combined hybrid figure (parked in HEV)→ICE
      the PHEV curve is left out, never drawn at zero
  • HEV or MHEV (full and mild hybrids — no plug)→ICE
  • petrol, diesel, gas, flex-fuel, others→ICE
